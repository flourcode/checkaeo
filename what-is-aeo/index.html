What is AEO?

Answer Engine Optimization, explained in four questions instead of acronyms.

What is AEOUpdated / 2026-09-30

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is the practice of making your website easier for AI-powered answer systems to reach, understand, extract and cite.

When someone asks ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Perplexity a question, the system often fetches live web pages, reads them, and composes an answer with sources. AEO is about making sure that when one of those systems lands on your page, it can actually use it.

That comes down to four plain questions.

1. Can AI reach the page?

Before anything else, the crawler has to load your page. That means a working URL over HTTPS, a 200 response, no noindex tag, a robots.txt that does not block the answer-engine crawlers (OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Googlebot, Bingbot), and real text in the HTML rather than a blank shell that fills in with JavaScript. Many AI crawlers do not run JavaScript. If your headline only exists after a script runs, they never see it.

2. Can it tell what you do?

An answer engine has a few seconds and a few kilobytes to decide what your site is about. It reads the title, the meta description, the H1 and the first paragraph. If those say "Welcome to the future" or "Crafted with care", it learns nothing. If they say "Quotabird is a set of free sales tools for reps and managers", it learns everything it needs. Clarity is the single most common problem we see, and it is the cheapest to fix.

3. Does the page answer questions directly?

People ask AI in questions, and AI answers by quoting. The most extractable pattern on the web is a heading phrased as a question, followed by a paragraph that answers it in 25–120 words and would make sense if copied out on its own. Definition sentences ("X is a …"), short lists and a small FAQ all help for the same reason: they are easy to lift accurately.

4. Is there enough context to cite it?

Before an engine attributes a claim to your site, it wants to know who you are. An About page, a contact path, an organization named in structured data, dates on articles, bylines where relevant, and links to sources or profiles all raise the chance that your page is treated as a source rather than an anonymous string of text.

What AEO is not. It is not a trick, a secret file, or a special tag. The emerging conventions (llms.txt, AI-specific meta tags) are worth knowing about, but none of them substitutes for a clear, reachable, well-organised page. If a tool tells you otherwise, it is selling something.

Questions people ask

What does AEO stand for?

Answer Engine Optimization. It is the practice of making a website easier for AI-powered answer systems, like ChatGPT search, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity, to reach, understand, extract and cite.

Is AEO the same as GEO or LLMO?

Different names for the same idea. GEO (generative engine optimization) and LLMO (large language model optimization) describe the same goal: content that AI systems can read and use. CheckAEO uses AEO because it is the most descriptive of the three.

Do I need to do AEO if my SEO is already good?

Mostly, good SEO covers it. The differences are emphasis: AI systems care more about a direct opening answer, self-contained paragraphs, and clear statements of who you are, and they read the raw HTML rather than the rendered page. See AEO vs SEO for the specifics.

Can AEO guarantee my site gets cited by AI?

No. Nobody can. AEO removes obstacles; it does not buy placement. Treat any tool that promises citations with suspicion.
