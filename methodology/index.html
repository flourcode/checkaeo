Methodology

How the AEO score works

Four weighted categories, every check, every point. Nothing hidden.

MethodologyUpdated / 2026-09-30

CheckAEO scores one page (usually your homepage) on four questions, each from 0–100, and combines them into one score. Every check below is a plain heuristic. Nothing is hidden and nothing is learned from your data.

What the scanner fetches

  • The page you enter, following up to five redirects, reading at most 1.5 MB, with a 12-second timeout. It identifies itself as CheckAEOBot/1.0.
  • /robots.txt, /sitemap.xml and /llms.txt at the final domain.
  • Response headers, including X-Robots-Tag.

It does not run JavaScript, log in, submit forms, or crawl other pages. It only requests public http and https URLs and refuses private or internal addresses.

The score

Overall = Access × 0.30 + Clarity × 0.25 + Answers × 0.25 + Trust × 0.20, rounded to a whole number.

Grades: A 90–100, B 80–89, C 70–79, D 60–69, F below 60. Status labels: Excellent (90+), Strong (80–89), Mostly clear (70–79), Needs work (60–69), Hard to understand (below 60), and Hard to reach when Access is below 40.

Gating rules. If the page does not return HTTP 200, Access is capped at 30 and the other three categories are marked "not evaluated", because what we received was an error page, not your content. If the page is noindexed, blocks all crawlers, or blocks three or more answer-engine crawlers, Access is capped at 45. If it blocks one or two, Access is capped at 65.

Answers (25%) — does the page answer questions directly?

The principle behind this category is: can the page provide clean, self-contained answers? The four core signals carry 75 of the 100 points. FAQs, question-shaped headings and definition sentences are supporting signals worth 15 points combined, and when a page already explains itself directly their absence costs only a few points. A clear product homepage should be able to score well here without an FAQ or a single question heading. We do not want every homepage to become "What is X? X is a…".

Not scored, but reported

llms.txt presence, image alt coverage, the html lang attribute, training-crawler blocks, and HTML size appear under "Also noted". They are useful context and carry zero weight, because their effect on answer engines is either unproven or unrelated to readability.

What AI sees

The summary on your card is our best reading of the page, chosen from the meta description, H1, first H2 and first paragraphs by scoring each candidate sentence for plain declarative structure, concrete words, mention of the site name, and position. Imperative sentences ("Get started", "Book now") and marketing adjectives are penalised. If no candidate clears the bar, the card says so: "Unclear" is a real result, and usually the most useful one.

Grounding rule. The summary is always a verbatim sentence from your page, and the card names where it came from (meta description, main heading, opening text). CheckAEO never rewrites, paraphrases or synthesises a description of what a business does. When confidence is not high, the summary is prefixed "Appears to be about:". Incomplete-but-accurate beats polished-but-invented.

Fixes

Every failed or partial check has a matching fix. The three shown first are the ones that cost the most points after weighting. Suggested text is built only from facts found on your page (site name, description, links). Anything we did not find is shown as a [bracketed placeholder] rather than invented.

Limitations, honestly

  • One page, one fetch, no JavaScript. That matches how most AI crawlers behave, but a site that renders client-side may look worse here than it does in Google.
  • Heuristics are heuristics. A brilliant page written in an unusual structure may score lower than it deserves. Read the checks, not just the number.
  • Nothing here predicts rankings, mentions or citations. It measures readiness: what an answer engine can and cannot get from the page.
  • Bot-protection services (challenge pages, WAF rules) will block our scanner just as they block AI crawlers. That result is informative, but the fix is on your hosting side.
