What is llms.txt?

A proposed reading guide for AI systems. Cheap to add, unproven in effect.

Convention noteUpdated / 2026-09-30

llms.txt is a proposed convention: a Markdown file at /llms.txt that gives AI systems a short, curated overview of your site and links to its most important pages.

It was proposed in late 2024 by Jeremy Howard of Answer.AI as a lightweight way to help language models find the substance of a site without wading through navigation, cookie banners and marketing pages. Think of it as a reading guide rather than an access rule.

What goes in it

# Your Site Name

> One paragraph that says what the site is, who it is for, and what it offers.

## Key pages

- [Pricing](https://example.com/pricing/): what it costs and what is included
- [Documentation](https://example.com/docs/): how the product works

## Optional

- [Blog](https://example.com/blog/): articles and updates

The format is plain Markdown: an H1 with the site name, a blockquote summary, then H2 sections containing link lists with one-line descriptions. Some sites also publish /llms-full.txt containing the full text of their documentation.

Does anything read it?

Honestly: as of 2026, there is no public confirmation that OpenAI, Anthropic, Google or Perplexity use llms.txt for search or answers. Adoption is mostly among developer-tool companies and documentation sites. It may become important; it is not important yet. That is why CheckAEO notes it but does not score it.

What matters more

A crawler that cannot load your page, or cannot tell what you do from the H1 and first paragraph, is not rescued by an llms.txt file. Fix access, clarity, answers and trust first. If you have those, adding llms.txt takes ten minutes and costs nothing.

Questions people ask

Does llms.txt affect my AEO score?

No. CheckAEO reports whether it exists under "Also noted" but gives it zero weight, because there is no public evidence that major answer engines use it yet.

Should I still add one?

It is cheap and harmless, and it forces you to write a one-paragraph description of your site, which is useful on its own. Just do not expect it to change what AI systems say about you.

Is llms.txt the same as robots.txt?

No. robots.txt controls access and is universally respected. llms.txt is a proposed content guide that crawlers may or may not read.
