What the checks rest on: crawler docs, web standards, and research.

ReferencesUpdated / 2026-09-30

The checks in CheckAEO are grounded in published crawler documentation, web standards, and the observable behaviour of answer engines. Here is what each part of the score rests on.

Crawler and robots documentation

  • OpenAI — "Overview of OpenAI crawlers" (platform.openai.com/docs/bots): defines OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User and GPTBot and their separate purposes.
  • Anthropic — Help Center article on Anthropic's crawlers: documents ClaudeBot (training), Claude-User and Claude-SearchBot.
  • Perplexity — docs.perplexity.ai crawler page: PerplexityBot and Perplexity-User.
  • Google — "Google's crawlers and fetchers" (developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing): Googlebot versus Google-Extended.
  • Microsoft Bing — Bing Webmaster Tools crawler documentation (Bingbot).
  • Robots Exclusion Protocol — RFC 9309 (IETF, 2022): the parsing and longest-match rules CheckAEO implements.
  • Google — "Robots meta tag, data-nosnippet, and X-Robots-Tag specifications": the noindex handling we check.

Structured data and page semantics

  • Schema.org — Organization, WebSite, Article, FAQPage, LocalBusiness and Person types.
  • Google — Structured data general guidelines and the FAQ / Organization feature documentation.
  • WHATWG HTML Living Standard — sections on the title element, headings, and the main, nav, header and footer landmarks.
  • Sitemaps protocol — sitemaps.org.
  • The Open Graph protocol — ogp.me (og:site_name).

Content quality and extractability

  • Google Search Central — "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" and the Search Quality Rater Guidelines (E-E-A-T), which inform the Trust category.
  • Nielsen Norman Group — research on how people read on the web (F-pattern, inverted pyramid), which supports answer-first structure.
  • Plain-language guidance — plainlanguage.gov and the UK GDS style guide, which support the sentence-length and vagueness checks.
  • Academic work on generative engines — "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization" (Aggarwal et al., 2023/2024) found that adding citations, statistics and clear structure increased visibility in generated answers; this supports the Answers and Trust checks, with the caveat that its findings are from experimental engines.

Emerging conventions

  • llms.txt — llmstxt.org, proposed by Jeremy Howard (Answer.AI), 2024. Reported by CheckAEO, not scored, pending evidence of adoption by major engines.

What we do not have a source for

No engine publishes its ranking or citation logic. Weightings in CheckAEO (30/25/25/20 and the points within each category) are editorial judgements based on the documents above and on hands-on testing, not on any engine's published formula. We revise them when the evidence changes, and note changes on the methodology page.

Links above are given as site and document names rather than deep URLs because vendor documentation moves frequently. Search the document title on the vendor's site for the current version.
